# Turkish IPO options: the discount-rate question (2000)

London Research: memory/domains/organization/london-research.md.

- [2000-06-19] John Bottomley (Enron Europe; sent by John Sherriff): Enron holds 116,000 options (9%) in a private Turkish company planning an IPO late Sep/early Oct 2000, strike $118.75 (34,800 expire 15 Jul 2000, the rest 15 Dec); its bankers (ABN AMRO, Rothschild) see about $300M against $118M implied; Enron proposes a higher strike (20% below the IPO price) and 3+ years. RAC values them in Crystal Ball (IPO and no-IPO cases, a put at a fixed 17% return, 30% illiquidity discount) at the company's 18% senior debt cost; London argues for the US risk-free rate. ←[[m02014]]
- [2000-06-19] Owner (cc Surbey, Gibner): "It seems to me that using a risk-free rate is not appropriate."; a longer answer promised, then on 21 Jun: "Please, give me another day to elaborate. It's quite hectic here." ←[[m02014]] ←[[m02051]]
- [2000-06-26] Owner's answer to Bottomley, run by Gibner and Shanbhogue ("to make sure we all agree"): "I think that illiquidity discount represents double counting when combined" with a project-finance rate (illiquidity discounts fit financial options in thin markets; this deal was valued like physical assets); a seller of an unhedgeable option takes Black-Scholes as the floor and prices like insurance (expected loss plus 1-2 standard deviations of unexpected loss); if Enron is short such an embedded option, subtract its value above Black-Scholes, else use a rate with a risk premium; buyers apply a discount. Insurance pricing was studied for Jere Overdyke (ask Shanbhogue); he doubts the deal reduces risk given Enron's positions in the region. ←[[m02108]]
