- [2001-03-16] Mark Wilson (cc Bradford) proposes Dun & Bradstreet's Financial Stress score for small exposures without financial statements, mapped through implied default rates to E-ratings for credit reserves (talks with Rabi, Amitava); D&B's Dan Barry on the owner's false-positive question: it gives a likelihood of distress, not yes or no. ←[[m06250]]
- [2001-03-29] Darrell Duffie (Stanford, 28 Mar): a Bank of America note has Enron default swaps ~80 bp wider than Enron asset swaps, why? Owner: "I shall send youan explanation later today or Monday."; 30 Mar: "I am sending you 2 technical notes on Enron default swaps"; Enron is "very slow in preparing a number of technical documents" for his reviews (London credit models, VaR, option pricing); "I still think we owe you money." ←[[m06421]] ←[[m06428]] ←[[m06430]]
- [2001-03-30] Seyfried forwards Martin McDermott's 23 Mar note: asset swaps and CDS carry the same senior unsecured risk, so the gap is supply and demand; scarce bonds keep asset-swap spreads lower; per JP Morgan, convertible hedgers pairing a cheap equity call with CDS bid CDS up; a fuller note to follow. ←[[m06436]]
