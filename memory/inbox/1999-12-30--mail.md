## 08:31

(mail · sent · m00125 · automatic capture)
From: Vince J Kaminski · Subject: Audit

- The owner (writing from VKaminski@aol.com, 10/03/99) commented on a draft for the Model Risk project, calling model risk very important and critical to Enron, since a wrong model leads to bad decisions, accounting errors and incorrect financial statements. (owner, verbatim: "I think it's a very important issue and really critical to Enron.")
- The owner's view: the main source of model risk at Enron is that responsibility for final model approval and validation is dispersed; no single unit or person has authority to audit, approve and monitor valuation models. He wants this told plainly to senior management. (owner, verbatim: "The popular perception is that this is done by the Research Group, but this could not be further form the truth.")
- The owner described the Research Group as having become a group of internal consultants working on highly specialized, partial problems, only rarely handling a project from A to Z. (owner, verbatim: "we became a group of internal consultants, working on highly specialized, partial problems")
- The owner suggested expanding the draft's point on inappropriate application of models with two sub-points: wrong inputs and the wrong model used for a transaction. He cited TVA as a transaction whose inputs had not been updated for an extended period. (owner, verbatim: "TVA is an example of point a")
- The owner noted that several audits of the Research Group's models by Arthur Andersen and Darrell Duffie were started at the Research Group's insistence. He suggested citing this under Control Strength. (owner, verbatim: "several audits of the Research Group model by Arthur Andersen and Darrell Duffie have been initiated at our insistence")
