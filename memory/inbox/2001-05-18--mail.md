From: Andrews, Naveen · Subject: RE: PLEASE READ -Analysis of variance between spreadsheet VaR and RisktRAC VaR for UK Power

- Naveen Andrews told Slava Danilov he prefers keeping the RiskTrac UK Power VaR (25MM) over the lower spreadsheet VaR, as a provision for back-end curve risk not reflected in P/L, and noted even the spreadsheet VaR backtests poorly.
- James New reported that London will now report the RiskTrac UK Power VaR (USD 25m, 1-day) instead of the spreadsheet VaR (~USD 13-14m); most of the jump comes from RiskTrac splitting positions into component curves (SG, N+P, NBP) with PPP as primary.
- Fergus Trenholme (London Risk Management) and Stig Faltinsen (London Research) led the UK Power VaR reconciliation and plan to work with Houston research on primary/secondary curve settings, correlations and factor loadings, sending regular updates.
- Research suggested SG (station gate) should become a primary curve for UK Power in RiskTrac, since 75% of the position sits on SG while PPP is set as primary; impact still to be quantified.
- UK Power backtesting: realised P&L losses exceeded VaR only about once in 300 days; NETA had been in operation only ~45 days, so correlation/factor loading data should be treated with care and fat-tail/jump risk assessed.

## 14:36

(mail · sent · m07936 · automatic capture)
From: Kaminski, Vince J · Subject: Var Issues

- On 2001-05-18 the owner drafted a memo on Enron's VaR models (sent to his AOL address). He argues that a good risk management system is critical to counter analysts' reports calling Enron a hedge fund. He wants the system's quality shown to outsiders through targeted disclosure. (owner, verbatim: "It is critical that we continue to improve our risk management systems")
- The owner says Enron's VaR system takes inputs from the trading desks or calibrates them to the market, not to history. As an immediate improvement, he proposes replacing inputs that don't come from the desks with market-based, forward-looking data. (owner, verbatim: "we should review inputs that are not based on the trading desks")
- The owner notes that forward vol curves posted by the desks don't match later forward price moves, so backtesting fails for some portfolios. He proposes a module that systematically compares the desks' posted forward vols with subsequent price movements. (owner, verbatim: "implement a module that compares systematically")
- The owner is concerned that no single person is responsible for VaR model calibration. The responsibility is spread across IT, RAC and Research, so inputs are updated inconsistently without quality controls. Some inputs are 2 years old. (owner, verbatim: "Some inputs used currently in the system are 2 years old.")
- The owner believes some recent VaR system changes tied up IT resources and were of marginal use, some showing questionable understanding of the mathematics. He says there is no systematic process for prioritizing projects with all concerned parties. (owner, verbatim: "some recent modification to the system tied up IT resources and were of marginal")
