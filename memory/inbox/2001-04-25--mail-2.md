From: Chonawee Supatgiat · Subject: Re: calculating bid-ask prices

- Chonawee Supatgiat argued to Rich DiMichele that movies, unlike energy commodities, carry high unique risk and low market risk, so customers of Enron's movie box-office trading market would trade mainly for diversification rather than hedging.
- Chonawee Supatgiat recommended that any Enron position in movie box-office futures be diversified across 15+ movies (per the consultant's view), avoiding positions on a small number of films.
- Chonawee Supatgiat proposed a 1-day bidding period (e.g. every Monday, cleared end of day) for his dynamic bid-ask pricing system, and plans to write a memo detailing the system and send it to Rich DiMichele.
- Rich DiMichele (Enron Communications) told Chonawee that movie-trading prices would be indicative only; Enron could take positions but would initially seek the other side, and generic products like movie slates may add liquidity.
- Rich DiMichele questioned Chonawee's dynamic bid-ask settlement: what happens with no buyers, and open orders left for several days; he asked whether other markets trade that way. Chonawee believes the method is his own new idea.
