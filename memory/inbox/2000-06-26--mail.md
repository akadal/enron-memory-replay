## 02:25

(mail · sent · m02108 · automatic capture)
From: Vince J Kaminski · Subject: Option Valuation

- The owner told John Bottomley that an illiquidity discount combined with a project finance discount rate is double counting; illiquidity discounts belong to financial options in thin markets, and the Turkish transaction was valued like physical-asset investments. (owner, verbatim: "I think that illiquidity discount represents double counting when combined")
- The owner's view: a seller of an unhedgeable option should treat Black-Scholes as the floor and price it like insurance (expected loss plus 1-2 standard deviations of unexpected loss), depending on risk preference and existing portfolio positions. (owner, verbatim: "Black-Scholes valuation becomes the floor for")
- The owner said that if Enron is short an unhedgeable option embedded in a deal, the option's value (above Black-Scholes) should be subtracted from the contract value; otherwise use a discount rate with a risk premium. Buyers should apply a discount instead.
- The owner said the research group looked into insurance pricing at the request of Jere Overdyke, and referred John Bottomley to Vasant Shanbhogue for more on it. (owner, verbatim: "We looked into insurance pricing at the request of Jere Overdyke.")
- The owner said he ran his option valuation response by Stinson Gibner and Vasant Shanbhogue to make sure they all agree; he doubts the Turkish deal reduces risk given Enron's positions in the region. (owner, verbatim: "I ran my response by Stinson and Vasant, to make sure we all agree.")
