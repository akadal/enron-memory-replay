From: Kaminski, Vince J </O=ENRON/OU=NA/CN=RECIPIENTS/CN=VKAMINS> · Subject: FW: Overview of Investor Conference Call

- On 2001-11-16 the owner forwarded to his personal AOL address a Nov 14, 2001 memo from Ken Lay (Office of the Chairman) to all Enron employees summarizing the investor conference call held that day.
- Ken Lay's memo said Enron split its businesses into three groups. Core: pipelines and gas, power, retail and coal in North America and Europe. Non-core: global assets and broadband, with over $8 billion invested and dismal returns. Under review: global and industrial markets.
- Ken Lay's memo said the business review will determine where Enron makes workforce reductions, with more information to follow. It blamed bad investments (Azurix, India, Brazil), over-leverage, related party transactions and the earnings restatement.
- Per Ken Lay's Nov 14, 2001 memo, Enron's credit ratings were: Moody's Baa3 under review for further downgrade, Fitch BBB- evolving status, S&P BBB- CreditWatch Negative. The 10-Q would be filed five days late, on Nov. 19.
- Ken Lay's memo said over $800 million of non-core assets were contracted for sale this year: CEG Rio, EcoElectrica and India offshore oil and gas. The ~$2.9 billion Portland General sale was expected to close in late 2002. A Dynegy-Enron joint proxy was to be filed.
