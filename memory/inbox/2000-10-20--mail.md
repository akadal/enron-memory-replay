From: Clayton Vernon · Subject: enron's new weather system, courtesy the research dept.

- Clayton Vernon told Vasant Shanbhogue (cc the owner) that he tested the Research dept's new real-time weather surveillance and messaging system, which delivers weather data to desktops more than 6 1/2 minutes before the National Weather Service updates its webpages.
- Clayton Vernon said the previous weather technology web-scraped National Weather Service pages via ftp and parsed them into a database; weather stations upload data around 53 minutes after the hour, and the new system receives it within 2 minutes.
- Clayton Vernon said the new weather system lets the nonlinear stack model be re-estimated before the top of the hour, when hourly power trading begins, instead of around 15 minutes after the hour, putting hourly power traders effectively one hour ahead in information.
- Clayton Vernon reported that people, from the CTO on down, are very excited about the new weather system, and thanked Vasant Shanbhogue for letting it happen.

## 10:06

(mail · received · m03754 · automatic capture)
From: Zimin Lu · Subject: transport model

- Zimin Lu told Andy (Andrew Lewis) that the scale/level effect in the transport model can be explained and the transport model still does what it should do; owner was cc'd along with Colleen Sullivan, Greg Couch, John Griffith, Stinson Gibner.
- Zimin Lu's illustration: raising underlying and strike by the same amount with fuel-percentage strike adjustment lowers intrinsic value, but option premium rises with level unless fuel percentage is very high (>8%).
- Zimin Lu noted that in transport deals fuel change is often below 5%, so spread option (transport) value does not decrease when NYMEX moves up.
- Zimin Lu's example used a European option with r=6%, vol=20%, T=100 days; a spreadsheet adding a 'NYMEX add-on' to delivery and receipt curves showed transport value (PV of spread options) increasing with the add-on.
