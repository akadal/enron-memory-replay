## 05:59

(mail · received · m00042 · automatic capture)
From: Clayton Vernon · Subject: (no subject)

- Clayton Vernon told the owner and Vasant Shanbhogue that he met Chris Germany, Capacity Trader at the East Desk, about gas transmission capacity, apparently as input to a model they are building.
- Clayton Vernon reported that pipelines typically lease capacity billed monthly as a fixed payment; example South Texas to Brooklyn at $12.00 per month per 10,000 decatherms ($0.40 per day).
- Clayton Vernon reported variable transmission charges of 6% for fuel (shrinkage) and 6.5% for overhead; a trader's willingness to pay for firm capacity equals the delivered-price spread minus variable costs (e.g. $0.35 - $0.125 = $0.225).
- Clayton Vernon reported that leaseholders know the firm-equivalent of interruptible capacity well, and many pipelines do not discount firm capacity from the tariff maximum; secondary markets are not yet important to the model.
- Clayton Vernon noted that gas from South Texas to Brooklyn can take several routes (Enron, Texas Eastern pipelines, etc.), and traders covering Enron positions at each end frees capacity for other contracts.

From: Kimberly Watson · Subject: Intelligence: El Paso Capacity--Someone has Upped the Ante But Match Still Possible

- Kimberly Watson forwarded to the owner (FYI) a Lorna Brennan ET&S intelligence memo, dated 12/16/99, on El Paso's negotiated deal for slightly more than 1.2 Bcf/d of firm capacity to the California border.
- Per the memo, El Paso confirmed a higher bid of $38 million for one year starting Jan. 1 topped the announced $30 million negotiated deal. The original negotiating party could match the new price to keep the capacity.
- A source in the memo said that on a mark-to-market basis the El Paso capacity is worth only about $20-25 million and called the $38 million bid 'ridiculous'.
- The memo reports speculation that Duke Energy was the original negotiated recipient and that Dynegy made the higher bid. A Dynegy source said Dynegy bid and lost to Duke in the first round.
- Per the memo, the Southern California border/San Juan basis spread fell from a December index spread of 29 cents to about 18 cents Wednesday. This was due to SoCal Gas storage withdrawals and cold-weather peaking calls.
