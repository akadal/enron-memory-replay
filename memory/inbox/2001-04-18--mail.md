## 08:54

(mail · received · m06965 · automatic capture)
From: Tomas Valnek · Subject: Data for Moody's RiskCalc

- Tomas Valnek told Iris Mack that, from initial work by him and Ben Parsons, data availability for Moody's RiskCalc is relatively good for European companies, but poor in low-reporting jurisdictions (e.g., the Netherlands), and newly established entities with no accounts cannot be priced.
- Tomas Valnek noted S&P has a similar credit model (www.creditmodel.com); the London team met S&P on 18/04/2001 and S&P will provide logins, which Valnek will forward to Iris Mack.
- Tomas Valnek's view: Moody's RiskCalc is likely more relevant than S&P's model, since S&P's is calibrated only on S&P-rated companies and has a high USD 100m threshold, while Moody's data is not restricted to firms it rates.
- Tomas Valnek warned that US private counterparties need not publish accounts, and that quality data is one of the key open issues, likely to determine which companies Enron can price and to influence model selection.
- Tomas Valnek asked Iris Mack (or whoever examines data sources/vendors for the RiskCalc credit model work) to keep Eric Kirkpatrick and Mike Mumford in the loop for full co-ordination.

(mail · received · m06972 · automatic capture)
From: Nelson Neale <Nelson Neale/NA/Enron@ENRON> · Subject: RE: Petrochemical Forward Curves

- Nelson Neale told the owner he met with Christian LeBroc last week about a fundamental/econometric approach to petrochemical forward curve construction, like the one used for the agricultural curves, and asked how the owner, Vasant and Stinson want to proceed.
- Christian LeBroc wants to work with Nelson Neale or someone in Research to build a forward curve model for all petrochemical products, modeled on the "Curve Model2K" built by Research a few years back for the Plastics trading group.
- Christian LeBroc's own "Benz_Curve" benzene forward curve model is, in his words, very primitive compared with the Curve Model2K.
- Christian LeBroc reported that supply, demand and utilization show no statistical relationship with aromatics (benzene) prices, even with time lags; he proposed using crude volatility for a GBM component.
- Nelson Neale noted that mean reversion and GBM models rely only on historical prices, and that GBM volatility is hard to estimate for petrochemicals with no forward/futures curve; the ag-curve approach adds supply and demand fundamentals.
