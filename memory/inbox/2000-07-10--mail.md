(mail · received · m02305 · automatic capture)
From: Zimin Lu · Subject: Re: Vol rollup

- John Disturnal asked Zimin Lu whether the implied volatility of the Dec00 NG contract with 30 days to expiry can be inferred from its current vol (66%) and term to maturity (143 days), to understand near-winter NG implied vols approaching expiration.
- Zimin Lu proposed splitting total variance: (66%)^2*143 = sigma1^2*113 + sigma2^2*30, where sigma1 is the vol for the first 113 days and sigma2 the vol of the Dec-00 contract in its last 30 days.
- Zimin Lu suggested using Nov-00 implied volatility as a proxy for sigma1, then solving for sigma2 = sqrt(((66%)^2*143 - sigma1^2*113)/30) as the Dec-00 vol in the last 30 days.

- The owner told Chonawee Supatgiat that short-selling Enron stock instead of exercising employee stock options may be a bad decision because of tax implications (ignoring legal aspects). (owner, verbatim: "short-selling the stock may be a bad decision because of tax implications (ignoring the legal aspects).")
- The owner's tax reasoning: option exercise income is taxed as ordinary income (28% in his example), but only $3,000 of a capital loss on the short position can offset ordinary income; the rest carries forward. (owner, verbatim: "You can use only $3,000 of your loss against ordinary income.")
- The owner regards valuation and optimal exercise of employee stock options as a very interesting and difficult problem. (owner, verbatim: "valuation and optimal exercise of employee stock options is a very interesting and difficult problem.")
