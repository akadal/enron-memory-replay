From: Vince J Kaminski · Subject: FYI Re: South Korea curve: FX and inflation, dated 1/8/2001

- Gwyn Koepke (for Maureen Raymond-Castaneda) sent Monica Reasoner an updated 20-year forward curve for the Korean won and Korean inflation dated 1/8/01; Monica asked for the assumptions because the SK-Enron team wanted their market views.
- Gwyn Koepke explained the South Korea FX curve assumes slightly faster won depreciation than consensus and the forward market at the short end, due to incomplete financial and corporate restructuring.
- Gwyn Koepke's view: Korean financial restructuring will take at least two years; stock market fell 50% since early 2000; restructuring cost may reach 26% of GDP; failure to sell Daewoo Motor and Hanbo Steel signals obstacles to reform.
- Gwyn Koepke argued a weak yen will make Korea's central bank cautious about defending the won, since Japan is Korea's main export competitor and exports are half of Korea's GDP; Korea's FX reserves are strong.
- Gwyn Koepke forwarded the South Korea curve rationale to the owner on 03/23/2001 (earlier to Cindy Hudler); the owner forwarded it to his AOL address. Main forecast risks: stalled reform, chaebol/bank resistance, or the government softening reform.
