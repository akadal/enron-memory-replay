(mail · sent · m06281 · automatic capture)
From: Vince J Kaminski · Subject: NYTimes.com Article: Companies Turn to Grades, and Employees Go to Court

- On 03/19/2001 the owner forwarded to David Oxley, marked FYI, a NYTimes article by Reed Abelson, 'Companies Turn to Grades, and Employees Go to Court', about forced-ranking employee evaluation systems. (owner, verbatim: "David,

FYI")
- Before forwarding it to David Oxley, the owner had emailed the same NYTimes forced-ranking article to someone named Jeff, marked FYI. (owner, verbatim: "Jeff,

FYI,")
- The NYTimes article reports discrimination lawsuits filed over about the last year against Microsoft, Ford Motor and Conoco. The suits challenge forced-ranking or bell-curve grading systems used to set pay and decide firings.
- Per the article, GE supervisors each year identify the top 20% and bottom 10% of managerial and professional staff. Ford grades 18,000 managers A/B/C, and last year gave 10% an A, 80% a B and 10% a C.
- Per the article, critics say a bell curve assumes a normal distribution across large random groups, not small ones, and that it penalizes talented units. Edward E. Lawler III called the results 'dysfunctional.'
