## 08:55

(mail · received · m06240 · automatic capture)
From: Kristin Walsh · Subject: California Update 3/15/01

- Kristin Walsh's California Update of 3/15/01 (to Lavorato, Nowlan, Kitchen) reported that DWR spends $40 to $60 M daily on power, is essentially bankrupt, and needs rate increases to securitize the planned $10B bond issue behind Davis' long-term contracts.
- Kristin Walsh reported that Davis–PG&E negotiations are stalled: PG&E wants 2.9 times book for its transmission lines (SCE accepted 2.3) and relief from its role as ultimate power purchaser; PG&E may put its utility subsidiary into bankruptcy if forced.
- Kristin Walsh reported that QFs/small generators are the most likely source of an involuntary bankruptcy filing against PG&E, SoCal Ed and SDG&E, with SB 47X (QF relief bill) held up in the State Senate.
- Kristin Walsh reported that the Bush administration favors replacing FERC's Hebert with Texas PUC head Pat Wood, while Trent Lott backs Hebert. The administration opposes price caps and is weighing privatizing federal assets such as BPA.
- Kristin Walsh reported that FERC would likely approve any Davis transmission deal, but with conditions. She also reported that Senate Pro-tem John Burton set up a special committee to investigate generators for price manipulation, and that the State Auditor plans an investigation.

(mail · received · m06249 · automatic capture)
From: Kristin Walsh · Subject: California Update 3/16/01

- Kristin Walsh reported (California Update 3/16/01) that sources say Coram Energy plans to file an involuntary bankruptcy against Socal early next week, corroborated via an intermediary with a senior CA ISO board member.
- Kristin Walsh described Coram Energy as a small wind generator QF based in West Vancouver, British Columbia, that also owns wind stations in California, with 238 40 kw units.
- Kristin Walsh's source believes the Coram filing was prompted by the view that a blackout may be needed for Governor Davis's bailout plan to pass the legislature; blackouts are unlikely in April or May, so cash-strapped QFs face a long wait for payment.
- Kristin Walsh reported that Coram reportedly has two other creditors (owed $10,000 or more) who will file with it; their identities are still being sought.
- Kristin Walsh reported that other QFs are said to be "taking legal advice" on how to proceed in light of the Coram developments.
