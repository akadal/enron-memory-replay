From: Vladimir Gorny · Subject: Price Caps

- Vladimir Gorny forwarded to the owner Tim Belden's 10/27/2000 memo on California price caps: on 10/26/2000 the ISO Board voted 13-10 for a new price cap methodology, effective 11/3/2000 or as soon after as possible (CAISO staff say that date is hard).
- Per Tim Belden's memo, the new CAISO caps use the NYMEX last-3-day settlement average times a heat rate by load level: <25 GW 10,775; 25-30 GW 14,175; 30-35 GW 17,225; 35-40 GW 27,225; >40 GW flat $250/MWh. Caps round up to $5; demand bids are exempt.
- Per Tim Belden's memo, FERC delegated cap authority to CAISO until 11/15/2000; the ISO filed for an extension, and FERC must rule by 11/18/2000. FERC would release its proposed 206-investigation order on 11/1/2000, with oral comments on 11/9/2000 in Washington.
- Per Tim Belden's memo, Enron had not yet decided who would give oral comments at FERC on 11/9/2000. He asked recipients not to share the attached price cap spreadsheet outside Enron.
- Belden's team estimated maximum monthly prices under the caps from 1999-2000 hourly loads: each hour priced at the implied cap across a range of gas prices, then averaged by month into peak and off-peak tables. It flags where 10/27/2000 forward power prices exceed the cap.

(mail · sent · m03963 · automatic capture)
From: Vince J Kaminski · Subject: Alliance Info Alert: FERC Report on Western Markets

- The owner forwarded to his personal AOL address an Alliance of Energy Suppliers (EEI) alert on FERC's 11/01/2000 report on the California summer 2000 power crisis. FERC found the state's market structure 'seriously flawed', leading to unjust and unreasonable rates.
- FERC's proposed California remedy, per the alert: drop the mandatory PX buy/sell requirement for utilities; schedule 95% of transactions day-ahead, with penalties for under-scheduling; independent non-stakeholder boards for the ISO and PX.
- FERC proposed a temporary $150/MWh soft cap on California's single price auction, with confidential weekly reporting to FERC of bids above $150. Price mitigation would run through December 31, 2002.
- FERC declined retroactive refunds but made sellers potentially liable for refunds on transactions from October 2, 2000 until December 21, 2002 if non-competitive behavior is found. Refunds would be no lower than marginal or opportunity cost.
- FERC asked for public comments on the California order by November 22 and set a technical conference for November 9. A final order is expected by year's end. Contact named in the alert: Jack Cashin, 202/508-5499.
