From: Vince J Kaminski · Subject: auctions with an incumbent's right of first refusal

- The owner sent Shelley Corman a summary by Clayton Vernon (who has an economics background) of arguments against an incumbent shipper's right of first refusal (ROFR) in auctions; cc Stinson Gibner and Zimin Lu. (owner, verbatim: "My colleague, Clayton Vernon, who has a background in  economics, wrote a short summary of arguments against the ROFR.")
- The owner told Shelley Corman his group is working on a numerical estimate of the value of the ROFR option, arguing the incumbent shipper holds something of value he never paid for. (owner, verbatim: "he has something  of value he never paid for.")
- The owner committed to produce for Shelley Corman a template for valuing the ROFR option case by case, since its value is case specific. (owner, verbatim: "so we shall rather produce a template you can use for valuation case by case.")
- Clayton Vernon's essay argues ROFR restrains free trade: competitors bid less or low-ball, the incumbent matches by rule of thumb without due diligence, and often keeps the contract at below-market price despite being less efficient.
- Clayton Vernon's essay also cites the winner's curse: a challenger who prevails because the incumbent fails to match has almost certainly overbid, so most competitors will not bother bidding.

- The owner forwarded the CERA alert 'Demand Overtakes Hydro in 2000' to Mike Roberts and Ronnie Chahal, telling Mike it is an area to focus on. (owner, verbatim: "Just got it this morning. An area to focus on.")
- CERA (Jan 20, 2000) expects western gas demand for power generation to rise by 600 MMcf/day on average in 2000 and up to 1,200 MMcf/day in Q3, as hydro returns to normal and demand grows.
- CERA expects gas prices to again set the floor for on-peak western power prices in 2000, with narrow western power differentials, higher power prices and more volatility as capacity margins tighten, especially in California.
- CERA expects Topock differentials to average about $0.25/MMBtu above Henry Hub in Q3 2000, versus $0.05 in Q3 1999; Topock-Malin spread expected at $0.13/MMBtu in February 2000.
- CERA expects the Rockies-San Juan differential to widen to over $0.15/MMBtu in summer 2000; Rockies differentials to Henry Hub to average $0.25/MMBtu in February 2000.
