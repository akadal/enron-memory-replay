## 12:13

(mail · sent · m00476 · automatic capture)
From: VKaminski@aol.com · Subject: Fwd: Mark-to-Market

- Sandeep Kohli asked Bob Butts (Jan 23, 2000) to review a mark-to-market treatment for an Enron India (EIPL) power transaction and send a note on how it would be marked to market under US GAAP.
- Sandeep Kohli said EIPL is structured through Mauritius and then the Cayman Islands, with Indian GAAP accounts in India and US GAAP consolidation in the US. He flagged a US tax accrual in the year of mark-to-market that India would not show.
- Pinnamaneni Krishnarao met Bob Butts and his colleague Wess (Jan 27, 2000). They said cash flows tied to one particular plant cannot be marked to market.
- Butts and Wess said the US workaround for peaking plants was to sell plant power to an outside party and buy it back at multiple unrelated locations, and that buyback is marked to market.
- Krishnarao said only 50% of DPC is owned by outsiders, so at most 50% of DPC cash flows could be marked to market. A simple loan to an affiliate cannot be marked to market. DPC selling to EIPL may be a poor option.
