From: Mark Wilson · Subject: FW: meeting follow-up

- Mark Wilson proposed that, absent objections, credit analysts begin using the Dun & Bradstreet Financial Stress score to underwrite small exposures where financial statements are not received, citing cost savings and greater consistency.
- Mark Wilson believes expected defaults from Financial Stress score ranges can be mapped to those implied in credit spreads, and thereby to E-ratings for use in credit reserve analytics; he has had preliminary discussions with Rabi and Amitava on this.
- Dan Barry of Dun & Bradstreet Analytical Services followed up a meeting that week with Mark Wilson's group (incl. the owner) on the Financial Stress Score, sending a presentation, country coverage, and performance charts.
- Responding to the owner's question on false positives, Dan Barry said the Financial Stress Score predicts likelihood of financial distress, not a yes/no outcome; some high-risk firms fail and some don't, as shown in the performance charts.
- Mark Wilson asked the owner, Rabi, Amitava, Teresa Seibel and Vasant Shanbhogue for any additional insights or information needs regarding the Financial Stress Score; William Bradford was copied.
