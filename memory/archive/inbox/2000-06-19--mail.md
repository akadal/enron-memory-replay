From: Benjamin Parsons · Subject: Pricing credit on thousands of names

- Benjamin Parsons told the trading group (owner cc'd) that the goal for the next few months is an automated system giving differential pricing on thousands of credits, 5,000 by year-end, most of them illiquid.
- Benjamin Parsons listed credit pricing models in use or under development: FMC model (default probability and credit spread approaches, Bloomberg Fair Market Curves), Bond model (FMC and exponential cubic spline approaches, under testing), CDS market prices, and KMV EDFs.
- Benjamin Parsons said KMV is being installed now and a default swap pricing model based on KMV Expected Default Frequencies will be built next.
- Benjamin Parsons proposed pricing illiquid credits as a weighted average of liquid market prices, with weights calibrated from credit analysis and financial ratios, adjusted periodically toward market/trader prices; neural nets/fuzzy logic seen as candidates.
- Benjamin Parsons noted McKinsey claimed such a credit pricing system would take 6-10 man-months to develop, and he asked the recipients for ideas and comments asap to set strategy.

## 06:52

(mail · received · m02021 · automatic capture)
From: Vasant Shanbhogue · Subject: Re: Pricing credit on thousands of names

- Vasant Shanbhogue proposed a starting approach for pricing credit on many names: for liquid names, apply the existing models (e.g., Ben Parsons' six) to a small set, compare with market prices and trader intuition, and identify attributes explaining dispersion.
- For illiquid names, Vasant Shanbhogue proposed applying the models, gathering trader views, then mapping illiquid names to a master set of liquid names via cross-sectional attribute analysis, so pricing could be automated from a table.
- Vasant Shanbhogue believes no single model will ever answer all questions for all names; mappings and model choices should be refined over time through ongoing round-table sessions with traders, in parallel with building new fundamental models.
- Vasant Shanbhogue stressed that limited data makes calibration very hard and suggested continually asking "What do we calibrate?" during the discussions.
- The credit pricing approach for thousands of names was to be discussed further in Tuesday's conference call (per Vasant Shanbhogue, 2000-06-19), with Ben Parsons, London credit trading, and the owner copied.
