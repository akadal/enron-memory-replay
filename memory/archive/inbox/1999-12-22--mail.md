From: Clayton Vernon · Subject: Re: working gas price model

- Clayton Vernon told Vince he has a simplified version of Brad's gas price model in mind. Its no-arbitrage condition equates trading margins across the country.
- Clayton's model assumptions: transmission costs rise with network congestion; wellhead supply is almost fully price-elastic; burner-tip demand is almost fully price-inelastic; storage is a perpetual call option.
- Clayton's model has 8 parameters per major node. Injection/withdrawal costs are least time-variant, then wellhead-to-pipeline delivery costs, then capacity-based pipeline transmission fees. Trading margins and storage option values are most time-variant.
- Clayton's model can estimate the 8 parameters per node two ways: from a short series of the last 3 days' prices, or by point calibration from today's data using a node-based model of competition between pipelines.
- Clayton will write up the simplified gas price model in Scientific Word and present it to Vince at his earliest convenience (no date given). Vasant Shanbhogue was copied.

From: Anjam Ahmad · Subject: New Volatility Curve Generator for UK Power

- Anjam Ahmad reported a new UK power volatility curve generator, down to EFA/monthly detail, marked to market up to 6 years out. Beyond that, volatility decays to an assumed long-term power volatility level.
- Per Anjam Ahmad, swaption traders can fit the first 5-6 years of the curve to market baseload swaption implied vols (typically 3-12 month underlying swaps), then price other swaptions, including individual EFA slot swaptions, consistently with it.
- Anjam Ahmad said the volatility curves reset as the market moves, enabling mark-to-market of the volatility book. The new curves may also affect the daily VaR calculation.
- The volatility curve spreadsheet model is saved at T:\READWRTE\ELEC_UK\MODELS\VOLCURVEGENERATORNEW.XLS and was sent to Houston staff for review.
