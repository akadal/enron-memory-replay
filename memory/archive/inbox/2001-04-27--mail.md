## 01:25

(mail · received · m07270 · automatic capture)
From: Sandeep Kohli · Subject: From The Enron India Newsdesk - April 27th newsclips

- Sandeep Kohli forwarded the owner the Enron India Newsdesk clips (compiled by Nikita Varma) of April 27th, 2001. They cover the Dabhol Power Company (DPC) crisis with MSEB. DPC is owned 65% by Enron, 10% each by GE and Bechtel, and 15% by MSEB.
- Indian press reported that the DPC board met in London on Wednesday and authorised Neil McGregor and K Wade Cline to serve a preliminary notice terminating the PPA with MSEB. The vote was six to one, with IDBI opposed. MSEB was not allowed to vote as an interested party.
- Press reports say DPC lenders are split. International lenders favour an Enron exit and are holding back phase-II disbursements. Indian lenders led by IDBI oppose termination because their phase-II exposure has no sovereign guarantee. Phase II is reportedly 92-95% complete.
- Per press reports, MSEB paid Rs 134 crore under protest toward the Rs 146.64 crore March bill and disputes Rs 12.64 crore. MSEB has also imposed a Rs 401 crore penalty on DPC that is heading to arbitration. DPC had said it would no longer accept protest payments.
- Press reports put the Centre's liability on termination at one year's power bill plus a $300 million termination fee. Power Minister Suresh Prabhu put it at Rs 2,840 crore. Under PPA clause 17.8, a preliminary termination notice triggers a six-month consultation period.
- Sandeep Kohli's Enron India newsclips of April 27, 2001 (part 2) report that the Dabhol Power Company board, meeting in London on Wednesday, empowered the DPC chief to serve a preliminary termination notice on the Maharashtra State Electricity Board.
- Per the April 27, 2001 newsclips, Maharashtra Chief Minister Vilasrao Deshmukh said a committee to renegotiate the Dabhol Power Purchase Agreement would be appointed, following the Godbole committee's suggestions, with a central government representative.
- Per the April 27, 2001 newsclips, Deshmukh called Enron's termination-notice decision unfortunate, said the only dispute with DPC was its expensive tariff, and said the project was still on.
- The April 27, 2001 newsclips name the DPC chief inconsistently: Mid Day says Neil McGregor, while Afternoon says Wade Cline. Afternoon adds that the project's lenders would have a decisive say on termination.
- Per the April 27, 2001 newsclips, State Energy Minister Dr. Padamsinh Patil said the state and central governments would form a joint committee to renegotiate with Enron, and that walking out would mean costly litigation for both sides.

From: Kristin Walsh · Subject: California Update 4/27/01

- Kristin Walsh's confidential California Update of 4/27/01 (to John Lavorato and Louise Kitchen) says the FERC price cap order is a Bush holding move that leaves the blame with Davis. It also says the administration opposes stronger caps because they would discourage new generation.
- Per Kristin Walsh's 4/27/01 update, the FERC order has a 'kill clause': the caps are withdrawn unless California's ISO agrees by June 1st to join the FERC-controlled regional grid.
- Kristin Walsh reported sources expecting California to pay as much as $20 billion for power this summer, more than the combined enterprise value of PG&E and SCE. The consensus is that the state runs out of money in about 90 days.
- Kristin Walsh reported that the QFs' request for advance payment from PG&E in bankruptcy court is likely to fail. If it does, most of the 4,000 MW of gas-fired QF capacity will likely stay offline, and new QF plants are likely delayed.
- Kristin Walsh reported that consumer groups (Harvey Rosenfield) are preparing a constitutional challenge to the SCE bailout MOU. The basis is that its non-bypassable charge is effectively a tax benefiting a private entity, so the bailout likely would not survive.
