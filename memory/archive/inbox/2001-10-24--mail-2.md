(mail · sent · m09578 · automatic capture)
From: VKaminski@aol.com@ENRON · Subject: Fwd: FW: Dynegy vs. Enron: A "Tale of Two Companies"

- On 2001-10-24 Paula Corey forwarded to the owner and Brian Mihura Will McNamara's article "Dynegy vs. Enron: A 'Tale of Two Companies'" (via Rhonda Brown of divine.com). The owner forwarded it to his AOL account.
- McNamara's article reports that Enron lost $638 million in 3Q 2001 after $1.01 billion in charges on non-core businesses, mainly broadband and water. Recurring net income rose 35% to $393 million (43 cents/share), and the stock was about $16, a six-year low.
- The article says Dynegy earned $286 million (85 cents/share) in 3Q 2001. It owns or controls about 27,000 MW and aims for 70,000 MW (10% of the US market) within five years. Enron owns or controls 2,015 MW.
- McNamara argues that asset ownership is the central difference between the two companies: Dynegy trades around physical assets, while Enron is asset-light. He also faults Enron's heavy broadband spending under Skilling and notes the SEC probe of Enron's CFO.
- McNamara expects a continued wide gap in Wall Street performance between Dynegy and Enron in the near term. He also says Enron's core wholesale business remains strong.
