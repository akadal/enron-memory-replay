## 02:35

(mail · received · m05969 · automatic capture)
From: Rabi De · Subject: Option pricing discrepancy for J.C. Penny Transaction

- Rabi De reported that Amitava and Seksan traced the J.C. Penney option pricing discrepancy between the credit-reserve model and the stand-alone deal-pricing spreadsheet to differing input variables, not algorithmic differences.
- Rabi De noted the credit-reserve simulation assumes monthly options expire on the first day of the underlying's contract month, which he considers a very reasonable assumption.
- Per Rabi De, the JC Penney deal spreadsheet assumed monthly options expire mid-contract-month, though the underlying monthly contracts actually expire before their contract months start; the spreadsheet leaves expiry validity to the user.
- Rabi De said Seksan is checking whether the JC Penney options are monthly options or a strip of daily options; if daily, intramonth volatility estimates would be needed for both the spreadsheet and credit reserve models.

- Sheridan Titman (Dept. of Finance, University of Texas at Austin) sent the owner his initial thoughts on determining discount rates for risky projects, with an attached document 'Determining discount rates for risky projects.doc'.
- Sheridan Titman told the owner he thinks risk capital should be defined simultaneously with the appropriate discount rate, and noted there is surprisingly little academic work on these issues.
- Sheridan Titman proposed modeling Enron's opportunity cost of capital and how it changes over time, arguing project liquidity strongly affects value and that duration and discount rates are likely related.
- Sheridan Titman suggested possible simulations to help quantify the discount rate and risk capital issues he raised.
- Sheridan Titman said he would call the owner later in the week of 2001-02-26 to discuss the discount rate document and asked the owner when he would be free.
