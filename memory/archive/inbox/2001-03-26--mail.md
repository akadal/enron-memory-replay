## 07:08

(mail · received · m06407 · automatic capture)
From: Tanya Tamarchenko · Subject: RE: forward prices simulations in the Credit Reserve Model

- Tanya Tamarchenko told Bill Bradford and Mark Ruane that simulating forward prices with the Credit Reserve Model's current methodology, from $5.2 gas at time 0 over 30 years, gives a 30-year price above $13.4 with probability 5% and below $22.1 with probability 99%; lower bounds $1.17 and $0.71.
- Tanya Tamarchenko's Credit Reserve Model simulation used the NG forward volatility curve, with 50% volatilities in the front and 13.5% vols for long-term contracts.
- Mark Ruane reported that, for a five-year swap, GBM-based simulation gives expected losses 18% higher than mean reversion in the Credit Reserve Model; he shared a chart (gbmcht.xls) comparing long-term gas prices under both processes.
- William S. Bradford said both GBM and reversion give fairly unrealistic values: $50 gas over the term seems improbable, but a $6 gas peak does not capture all potential price movement at a 99% confidence interval.
- William S. Bradford asked the team (Ruane, Andrews, Rohauer, Brackett, Tamarchenko, De, Jia) what assumptions they used on price curves, volatility curves and trend reversion in the Credit Reserve Model simulations.
