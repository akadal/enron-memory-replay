## 09:06

(mail · received · m01255 · automatic capture)
From: Joseph Hrgovcic · Subject: CME and CATEX

- Joseph Hrgovcic forwarded the owner a Business Insurance article (03/20/2000, Gavin Souter) on slow trading in exchange-based insurance products. Lucy Ortiz had sent it to him under the subject 'CME and CATEX'.
- Per the article, the CME began offering weather derivatives in September 1999, and 420 futures contracts had been traded, mostly between dealers. Heat indexes started with Atlanta, Chicago, Cincinnati and New York; more cities and cold-weather contracts were added in January.
- Per the article, CATEX had completed about 450 trades since inception, totaling $400 million in premium and over $3 billion in limits, with roughly 160 subscribers. It is now chiefly a 'cash for cover' reinsurance exchange.
- Per the article, no CBOT catastrophe option trades had been completed so far in 2000, and the Bermuda Commodities Exchange was suspended last year after little activity. Observers blame the soft traditional reinsurance market.
- Per the article, Morton Lane of Gerling Global Financial Products said cat exchanges lack enough product diversity to hedge. He suggested adding satellite, aviation and crop index options.
